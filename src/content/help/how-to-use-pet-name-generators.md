The name generators hold 100+ curated names for dogs and cats, each with its meaning and a style tag, filterable by gender and vibe. Everything runs in your browser, needs no sign-up, and saves your favorites locally on your device. This guide covers every control, how the shortlist works, and the naming principles behind the list.

## Using the generator, step by step

1. Open the [dog name generator](/tools/dog-names) or the [cat name generator](/tools/cat-names) — the controls are identical.
2. Filter by **gender** if it matters to you: male, female, or any. Every name carries a gender tag.
3. Filter by **style**: cute, cool, classic, funny or food. The style tag reflects the vibe the name gives off — "Biscuit" is food and cute; "Thor" is cool; "Pickles" is funny.
4. Browse the grid. Each card shows the name and a short meaning or inspiration ("Luna — the moon").
5. Tap **Shuffle** to re-deal the whole grid when you want fresh eyes.
6. Tap the **heart** on any name you like. It jumps into your shortlist at the top of the page.
7. Compare your shortlist, sleep on it, come back — saved names persist in your browser (per species, so dog and cat lists stay separate).
8. Use **Clear all** when the debate is finally settled. Congratulations.

## How the shortlist works

Favorites are stored in your browser's local storage — nothing is sent to a server, and no account is involved. That means your shortlist is tied to this device and browser: clear your browser data (or switch browsers) and the list resets. If you've fallen in love with a name, write it somewhere real before clearing anything.

The shortlist is intentionally simple — names plus meanings, no notes or sharing. The point is narrowing to three or four candidates you can say out loud for a day and see which one sticks.

## Choosing well: the principles we curated by

- **Two syllables is the sweet spot.** Short names are easiest for a pet to recognize and for you to shout across a park.
- **Bright "ee" endings carry.** Kiki, Mochi, Rosie — cats in particular respond well to these sounds.
- **Avoid command collisions.** "Kit" sounds like "sit"; "Bo" sounds like "no". If it's confusing to hear, it's confusing to learn.
- **The field test.** Imagine calling the name at full volume in a public park. If you'd hesitate, keep browsing — there are a hundred more below your scroll.

## Troubleshooting

**The grid shows very few names.** Two filters are probably combined too tightly — "female + food" gives you a handful by design. Reset one filter or hit Shuffle.

**My saved names disappeared.** The shortlist lives in this browser. Private/incognito windows don't keep it, and clearing site data removes it. Nothing was lost on our side — nothing ever left your device.

**I want more names.** The list is curated rather than infinite: every name here passed the "would we say it out loud" test. Shuffle re-orders, and we add new names regularly.

**Do meanings work in my language?** The name meanings are short English tags, kept universal across languages on purpose — the *names* are the same international shortlist everywhere.
