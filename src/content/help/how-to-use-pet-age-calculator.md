The pet age calculator converts your cat or dog's age into human years and tells you their life stage — baby, young, adult or senior. It runs entirely in your browser, needs no sign-up, and includes a full conversion chart you can scan at a glance. This guide explains every input, the math behind the result, and how to read the chart.

## Using the calculator, step by step

1. Open the [age calculator](/tools/pet-age).
2. Choose the **pet kind**: cat or dog. The formulas differ (see below), so pick first.
3. Enter the **age** in years. Half-years work — a 6-month-old puppy is `0.5`.
4. For dogs, choose the **size**: small (up to 10 kg), medium (11–25 kg) or large (over 25 kg). Size changes the result meaningfully — bigger dogs age faster after their early years.
5. Read the big result: human-years equivalent, plus a life-stage chip underneath.
6. Scroll to the **conversion chart** to see the whole curve from 0 to 16 — your pet's current row is highlighted automatically.

## The math behind the numbers

**Cats** mature fast, then settle: the first year counts as about 15 human years, the second adds roughly 9, and every year after adds about 4. A 3-year-old cat is a 28-year-old human — adult, in their prime.

**Dogs** follow the same early pattern (first year ≈ 15, second ≈ +9) but diverge by size afterward. Small dogs add about 4 human years per dog year, mediums 5–6, and large breeds 6 or more, growing with age. That's why a 7-year-old Chihuahua is middle-aged while a 7-year-old Great Dane is already a senior — and why the calculator asks for your dog's size.

One number everyone knows is wrong: "one dog year equals seven human years" is a myth. Dogs blow through their early years far faster than that, then slow down — the straight ×7 line fits no part of the curve.

## Reading the life stages

- **Baby** — under 1 year. Growth, chaos, and the zoomies.
- **Young** — roughly 1 to 3. Adult energy, still learning the rules.
- **Adult** — the long prime. For cats this runs to about 10; for dogs it depends on size (small dogs to ~10, medium to ~8, large to ~6).
- **Senior** — the golden years. More sleep, more vet attention, same love.

For a deeper version of this topic — including what changes when your pet reaches senior status — the [AKC's guide to dog years](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/how-to-calculate-dog-years-to-human-years/) is an excellent external read, and your vet can pin down your individual pet's aging with a physical exam.

## Troubleshooting

**My result differs from another website.** No single official formula exists — every calculator makes approximation choices. Ours follows the widely used veterinary approximation (15 / +9 / +4 per year for cats, size-weighted tables for dogs) and says so openly. Treat every result as a good estimate, not a lab value.

**My pet is older than 16.** Just type the number — the calculation continues past the chart's edge using the same yearly increments.

**Can I enter months?** Use decimals: 6 months is `0.5`, 18 months is `1.5`. The chart highlights the closest whole year.

**Why does size matter so much for dogs?** Large breeds literally live faster — their cells age quicker, which is why their senior years arrive earlier. It's the biggest single correction any dog-year formula needs.
